# Materials and paint: the game against the editor

Measured on Ballest of Them All 0.4.1 through the game driver (`tools/driver`, slot 6) on 2026-11-04. The per-piece numbers are also in `tools/extract/paint-measured.json`, and `tools/extract/src/paint.test.ts` holds `assets/catalog.json` to them.

## Result

The game's Paint panel is simpler than the catalog's slots suggested, and stricter than the app was.

- Every paintable piece offers the same library on every slot. No piece narrows the choices.
- 120 of the 140 palette pieces take paint. Each shows exactly one row per slot of its Main mesh, so `materialSlots` was right for them, outline slots included.
- The other 20 palette pieces and all 96 hidden pieces tried show no rows. The app offered paint on all of them.
- Most of those locks are only the panel's. A map file can paint 92 of the 100 locked classes that have slots: the game draws it in a race, saves it back, and the map validates. The app offers those slots with an "Off-panel" badge.
- A surface hides paint for the whole selection. The app had no such rule.
- What the app writes for a painted slot is what the game writes, string for string.

## The library

`BP_BaseItem` points `PDA_MaterialLibrary` at `MaterialCategories_Library`, a `PDA_MaterialCategories_C`. All 236 pieces measured read that same asset. It holds one array, `BallestMaterialCategories`, of seven `PDA_MaterialCategory_C` assets under `/Game/Art/DataAssets/BallestMaterials/`:

| Finish   | Asset                        | Colours |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | ------- |
| smooth   | `MaterialCategory_Smooth`    | 10      |
| rough    | `MaterialCategory_Rough`     | 10      |
| glossy   | `MaterialCategory_Glossy`    | 10      |
| concrete | `MaterialCategory_Concrete`  | 10      |
| tiled    | `MaterialCategory_Tiled`     | 10      |
| emissive | `MaterialCategory_Emmissive` | 10      |
| special  | `MaterialCategory_Special`   | 5       |

That is 65 materials, not the 66 our docs used to say. The six ordinary finishes use White, Black, Red, Orange, Yellow, Lime, Blue, SkyBlue, Purple and Wine, in that order. `assets/materials.json` `paint` has them all.

Each category has four maps and a name:

- `ColorMaterialMap`: `EBallestMaterialColors` to the material. Its keys are the swatches.
- `ColorColorMap`: the swatch colour. The ordinary finishes don't save one, so they inherit `Default__PDA_MaterialCategory_C`'s. `special` saves its own.
- `ColorPreviewTextureMap`: empty in every category.
- `MaterialCategory` and `MaterialName`. The name is what the dropdown shows, typo included: the game says "emmissive". We keep the enum's spelling in the app.

`EBallestMaterialColors` value 0 is Grey, which matters below. An item keeps its picks in `ActiveBallestMaterials` (the category per slot) and `ActiveMaterialColors` (the colour per slot). On load, `OnMaterialLoaded` rebuilds them by asking each category `ContainsMaterialInterface` for mesh component 0's materials. That is why a loaded `MI_Pipes` tube opens on "special", and why a piece whose default is in the library opens on that finish.

## How the panel decides

From the Blueprint bytecode (`W_ItemsDatas`, `W_ItemMaterialData`, `W_ItemMeshData`, `W_MeshMaterialData`, `WBP_CustomizeMaterial`), read with the build's own usmap and CUE4Parse's `ReadScriptData`, then checked in game.

1. `CreateItemList` walks the selection into a map keyed by class. An item counts only if it is a `BP_BaseItem_C` with `bCanChangeMaterial`. Later items of a class add to its count; the rows show the first item's data.
2. `W_ItemMaterialData` makes a `W_ItemMeshData` per mesh component, which makes a `W_MeshMaterialData` per material. Every piece has one mesh component in the game's data (9767 of 9767 in `samples/`, and all 142 items the game saved here).
3. `SetMaterialSlots` makes the row, but only if `DetermineItemDisplayName` finds the class in `DT_MapItems`, the table behind the palette, and `BallestSurfaceAssignmentLibrary.AreSelectionMaterialSlotsEditable` says yes. The first test is why a hidden piece with `bCanChangeMaterial` set shows nothing. The second is native; the only thing seen to turn it off is a surface on any selected item.
4. `SetMaterialListFiltered` is empty, so nothing filters the library.
5. A row is `SlotNumber = MaterialIndex + 1`, a dropdown from `SetupCB` in library order, and swatches from `GetAvailableColors`.
6. A swatch click calls `W_MeshMaterialData.SetMaterial`, which loops the selection, takes items of the same class, and calls the handler's `ApplyMaterial` with one `SKGMLEMaterial`, then `SetActiveBallestMaterial`.
7. Changing the dropdown sets the row's category and re-creates the swatches. If the row's colour is among them, it presses that swatch. The colour of a row that has none is 0, Grey. So on a slot off the picker, `special` paints `MI_Pipes` and the other six finishes paint nothing.

The dropdown only reacts to key, navigation and mouse selections, so a driver has to call its event with a `SelectInfo` of 2. `SetSelectedOption` alone does nothing.

A painted slot, as saved:

```json
  "materials": [
    {
      "material": "/Script/Engine.MaterialInstanceConstant'/Game/Art/Materials/Environment/Materials/Instances_2/MI_Env_Yellow_Tiles.MI_Env_Yellow_Tiles'",

  "materialAssetPaths": [
    "/Game/Art/Materials/Environment/Materials/Instances_2/MI_Env_Yellow_Tiles.MI_Env_Yellow_Tiles"

```

Every material the game saved from the panel equals its `paint` entry in `materials.json` exactly, and `setSlotMaterials` keeps `materialAssetPaths` in step the same way. Untouched pieces match too: the game's saved mesh and materials for 140 of the 141 classes equal what `itemFromPiece` writes. The exception is the decal, below.

| Test                                      | Result                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Paint slot 3 of a four-slot piece         | Only that slot changes                                                                 |
| Paint an outline slot (`M_OutlineEdges`)  | Saved like any other slot                                                              |

| Paint, then mirror                        | Paint kept, mesh swaps to the bake, same rows                                          |
| Mirror, then paint                        | Same rows, paint saved on the bake                                                     |

| Finish change, colour not in it           | Glossy Red to special keeps `MI_Env_Glossy_Red`; Copper to rough keeps `MI_Env_Copper` |

Map files the game was given, then saved again:

| In the file                                                   | What the game does                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Non-library material on a paintable piece (`MI_ArenaOrange2`) | Keeps it; the row shows smooth                                                                           |
| Paint on a locked piece                                       | Depends on the slot, see Off-panel paint                                                                 |
| Empty `materials`                                             | Fills in the defaults                                                                                    |

9767 mesh components in `samples/` have no entry past their mesh's slots, so the new save check has no false positive there.

Two traps for the next probe. A piece outside the placement bounds saves `M_LevelEditorHighlight` in slot 1, the invalid-piece marker. And saving from the probe map writes a second file, `driver probe_<creator>.balledit`, which also matches `openmap driver probe`, so delete it before the next `probe_map`.

## Off-panel paint

Off-panel paint is paint the game's panel won't set but a map file can. The name avoids "hidden", which the app already uses for pieces outside the palette.

A slot counts only if all of these held, on a map with every slot of every locked class painted:

- Racing the map (`Map_TrackUGCPlayback`), the slot's live mesh held glossy red, and in a second pass emissive orange. Six boxes and cylinders become instanced there, so I read their `BallestUGCPlaybackInstanceHost` components instead.
- The game saved the paint back unchanged.
- The map passed a real validation run: `StartValidationRaceFromCallback`, ball rolled into the goal, `bValidated` true and medal times `[7, 5, 5, 3.83]` in the saved file.

Of the 100 locked classes with slots, 92 have off-panel slots, 13 of them in the palette. Pictures of the 15 palette pieces with slots, taken during a race, agree with the live reads. The goal's ring shows red on the finish screen.

| Pieces                                           | What a map file's paint does                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Rubber, glass                                    | Replaces the whole look, so the glass goes opaque                                                         |
| Light cards                                      | Replaces the beam with a solid shape; the glow is gone                                                    |
| Laser, goal                                      | Paints the piece. The beam is separate and stays                                                          |
| Elevator                                         | Paints the base. The platform is another mesh with no slots here                                          |
| Spinning cylinder                                | Slot 1 paints. The game overwrites the cap (slot 2) with `MI_SpinningCylinderCap_Normal`, in the file too |
| Paddle                                           | Slots 3 and 4 paint. The game overwrites slots 1 and 2 with its speed materials, in the file too          |
| Point, spot and rect lights, both light fixtures | Nothing. The file keeps the paint, but the marker draws `DefaultMaterial`                                 |
| `BP_Surface_Grippy`, `_Ice`, `_Magnet`           | Nothing. The file keeps the paint, but the piece draws its surface                                        |

`BP_TwoCubeOneColor` and `BP_TwoCubeTwoColor` make the game discard the map with a mesh component at all, unpainted too (`assets/verified.json` already calls them rejected), so they have no off-panel slots. With no mesh component they load.

`tools/extract/paint-measured.json` `offPanel` holds the slots, and `build-catalog.mjs` copies them to `catalog.json` `offPanelPaint`. It has to be a table: the rule lives in native code and in each class's scripts, and nothing in the pak says which slots survive.

A surface still locks these slots in the app, since the game draws the surface over the paint.

## The catalog

`catalog.json` gains `paintable`: in the palette, and `bCanChangeMaterial` not saved false. 120 pieces are true and 118 false. With that, `materialSlots` are the game's paintable slots and `materials.json` `paint` the choices for each. The other catalog fields are unchanged by the regeneration.

Two facts need tables, because the extractor can't see them:

- Light cards. Their CDOs save `bCanChangeMaterial` false, but after `LightCardVisualContract`, a native struct missing from the Fortnite usmap `run.sh` downloads, so the read stops first. With the game's usmap, these four are the only classes that gain a saved false. `build-catalog.mjs` names them.
- The `special` order. The game shows it reversed from the cooked map, though `GetAvailableColors` is a plain `Map_Keys`. The other six finishes match the cooked order. `SWATCH_ORDER` in `src/materials.ts` holds the measured order. I don't know why it reverses.

## Not verified

- The picker on every piece. Each row showed 7 finishes and the default finish's 10 swatches, but I only cycled all the finishes on one piece. The empty `SetMaterialListFiltered` is the reason to expect the rest match.
- "emmissive" swatch order. My sweep sent the wrong spelling, so its order comes from the cooked map plus one swatch checked in game (index 3 is orange).
- `AreItemMaterialSlotsEditable` is native. A surface turns it off; it could have other conditions I didn't hit.
- Pieces went in through `Editor::SpawnPieces`, as a paste does, not by dragging from the palette. I expect no difference but didn't test one.
- Colours are the panel's `DisplayColor` values. I didn't compare them with a picture of the panel.
- A mixed selection's finish change follows the first item in the bytecode; I didn't test it in game, and the app's copy of that rule rests on the bytecode.
- The app itself wrote no map loaded here. The test maps came from a script writing the same fields, and the format evidence is the string comparison above.
- Off-panel paint on Workshop maps. The sandbox blocks Steam, so nothing was published. A validated local map is as far as I got, and a published `.ballmap` may go through checks I couldn't reach.
- Off-panel looks beyond the 15 pictured palette pieces. For hidden pieces I have live material reads, not pictures, and slots run at the driver's low settings.
- Off-panel materials beyond glossy and emissive, and slots painted one at a time. Every test painted all of a piece's slots together.
- The app's paint panel wasn't run in a browser. The rules have unit tests; the React wiring has only typecheck and lint.
- The 97th hidden piece, `MP_PinballPaddle`, is gone from the game. The player start showed no rows.
